1. Which months are worst and best?

February is the worst month, with an average PM2.5 level of 141.87 µg/m³. July is the best month, with an average of 27.09 µg/m³. February’s average is about 5.24 times higher than July’s.

2. What were the two highest days?

The highest reading was 320.0 µg/m³ on 8 November 2025. The second highest was 245.0 µg/m³ on 18 May 2025. The dataset does not tell us what caused these spikes.

3. How many days exceeded 15 and 75 µg/m³?

Out of 356 recorded days, 355 days (99.7%) were above 15 µg/m³, and 188 days (52.8%) were above 75 µg/m³.

4. When do unhealthy days build up fastest, and when does the curve go flat?

Unhealthy days build up quickly in January, February, November, and December. The curve stays flat during June, July, and August because there were no days above 75 µg/m³ in those months.

5. Does the suburb follow the city?

Yes. The suburb follows almost the same pattern as the city, but city readings are higher in every month. The largest monthly average difference is in February: 141.87 µg/m³ in the city and 109.2 µg/m³ in the suburb.

6. Which months had Severe days? Which had the most Low days?

Severe days occurred in January, February, March, May, October, November, and December. December had the most Severe days, with 11. July had the most Low days, with 28.

7. Is pollution linked to weekday, month, or both?

The monthly pattern is much stronger than the weekday pattern. Pollution is generally higher in winter and lower in summer. The weekday averages are closer to one another, so the month appears to have a stronger effect in this dataset.

8. Which month had the widest gap between its best and worst day?

November had the widest range. Its highest reading was 320.0 µg/m³ and its lowest was 80.9 µg/m³, giving a range of 239.1 µg/m³. May had the second-widest range.

9. Is there a long-term rise or fall? How many days are missing?

The data shows a seasonal pattern rather than a steady rise or fall. Pollution is high near the beginning and end of the year and lowest around June and July. There are 9 missing days, from 12 to 20 March 2025.

10. What can we see when we zoom into November?

The zoomed chart makes daily changes easier to see. It shows the very high reading of 320.0 µg/m³ on 8 November, compared with the other readings in that month.

11. What could a pie chart not tell the Director?

A pie chart cannot show the order of daily readings or when sudden spikes happened. It also cannot show the difference between the highest and lowest days or clearly show missing dates.

12. Which two months should have outdoor school restrictions?

February and December should be considered. February had the highest monthly average of 141.87 µg/m³, while December had the second-highest average of 138.81 µg/m³ and the most Severe days, with 11.
